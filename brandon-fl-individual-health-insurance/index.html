I Missed Open Enrollment — Am I Locked Out Until November? Not necessarily. A Special Enrollment Period opens on qualifying events like losing coverage, a qualifying move, marriage, or a birth or adoption. The important detail people miss: the 60-day window starts the day your prior coverage ends, not the day you start shopping, so a job that ended six weeks ago is a clock already running. Call before you assume you are stuck, and bring the coverage-termination date with you.
How Do I Know a Health Insurance Agency Is Not Just Steering Me? Ask two questions. First, which carriers can they write — an independent advisor compares across major carriers rather than one company's shelf. Second, who pays them, and does it change by plan. We are compensated by the carrier at the same rate the plan already builds in, which is why there is no fee to work with an agent and no reason to push you toward one product. Then ask them to show you the comparison itself, not just the recommendation.
Can I Switch Plans if My Doctor Leaves the Network Mid-Year? A mid-year network change is frustrating and usually does not on its own open a Special Enrollment Period, which is exactly why verification before enrollment matters so much. If it happens, call us — there are sometimes continuity-of-care provisions, appeal paths, or in-network alternatives worth reviewing before you assume you are stuck paying out-of-network rates.
