Picking the Right Plan — Common Questions

Five common questions that come up in nearly every first consultation. If yours isn't here, Sofia walks through it on your initial call.

What's the difference between an HMO and a PPO?

An HMO is the lower-cost path — you pick a primary care doctor inside the network and need a referral before seeing most specialists, so monthly premiums and copays tend to be lower. A PPO is the flexible path — you can see any doctor in or out of network, no referral required, in exchange for a higher monthly premium. Sofia recommends a PPO for most Tampa residents who already have specialists they want to keep, and an HMO when budget is the top priority and the household is comfortable working through a primary care physician.

Can I get health insurance if I'm self-employed?

Yes — self-employed individuals qualify for marketplace plans, and many end up on PPO coverage for considerably less than they expect. Depending on your reported income, premium tax credits can reduce your monthly cost significantly, and your insurance premiums are often deductible on your taxes as a self-employed business expense. Sofia maps your income and household to plans available in your ZIP code so you can see real numbers before deciding.

Is COBRA worth keeping after leaving a job?

COBRA lets you keep the exact employer plan you had — same network, same provider relationships — but you now pay the full premium plus an administrative fee, which is usually two to three times what your paycheck was covering. Sofia compares your COBRA cost against marketplace plans in your ZIP code before recommending a direction, because in many cases an ACA plan delivers comparable coverage at meaningfully lower monthly cost during the 60-day Special Enrollment window.

How much does health insurance cost in Tampa?

Premiums vary by age, household income, plan type, tobacco use, and the specific carriers available in your ZIP code. Many Tampa residents qualify for premium tax credits that dramatically reduce monthly costs — often dropping a marketplace plan from "expensive" to genuinely affordable. The only way to know your real number is to run an actual comparison with current carrier rates, which Sofia does on the first consultation at no charge.

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

It depends on the plan type. PPO and EPO plans typically let you book a specialist directly — no referral required — though EPO coverage is limited to in-network providers. HMO and POS plans usually require a referral from your primary care physician before specialist visits are covered. If you already see specialists regularly, that's a factor Sofia weighs heavily when narrowing your plan options.
